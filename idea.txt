# Banana — AI-Assisted Weekly Meal Planner

### Approved release-workflow amendment

Versioned plans in `release-plans/<version>` and verified notes in
`release-notes/<version>.md` replace new entries in the legacy release-plan and
hardcoded What's New files. Opening What's New explicitly refreshes public
notes from the official GitHub releases, with a persistent offline cache and an
installed-version cap. This approved network use is independent of automatic
update settings; it sends no personal data and never refreshes at startup.
See `AGENTS.md` and `release-plans/README.md` for the ordered implementation and
separate, user-invoked `--github --agent-notes` release procedure.

Banana is a private, local-first Android app that plans your next week of
meals, tells you what to buy, and spends your food budget where the deals are.
Its defining interaction is a **week**: you set how much time you have on each
day and anything you specifically want to eat, and Banana proposes a plan that
fits both, then turns it into one organised shopping list.

It is the sibling of **Coconut** (`../Coconut`, a budget app) and **Cherry**
(`../PassionFruit`, a coffee journal). All three share a family: the same
Material Design 3 foundation, the same architecture, the same offline-first
discipline, the same screen density, and the same habit of turning a vague
intention into a deliberate, judged plan. Banana is what happens when that
design philosophy is pointed at what you eat.

Read `AGENTS.md` for how to build it. Read `theme.md` for how it looks. This
document defines what it is.

## 1. Purpose

Banana solves one problem: deciding what to cook and then buying exactly the
right things is repetitive, thankless work, so it is usually done badly — the
same five meals, whatever is on the shelf, and a mid-week trip for the one
ingredient you forgot.

The app exists to answer three questions well, every week:

1. **What should we eat?** A plan that respects your time, your tastes, your
   diet, and your budget.
2. **What do we need to buy?** One shopping list, grouped, de-duplicated, and
   aware of what you already have.
3. **Where is the best value?** Deals at Lidl, Hofer and Spar, matched to the
   things the plan actually calls for.

The loop is:

```
set the week  ->  plan meals  ->  shop  ->  cook  ->  rate  ->  learn  ->  plan
```

Every feature either shortens the distance from "it is Sunday evening" to "here
is the shopping list", or it increases the quality of the next plan. Anything
that does neither is a non-goal.

The app is a **personal tool**, not a restaurant POS, not a nutritionist, not a
delivery service, and not a social network. One user, one device, one local
database.

## 2. Design Lineage: Coconut and Cherry

Banana, Cherry and Coconut are one family. Banana shares the most with Cherry,
because Cherry already solved the problem of an AI call whose output is a
*candidate the user confirms*, never a write.

### 2.1 What is shared

- **Material Design 3** foundation and the same `buildTheme` / token
  construction (`theme.md` §2, §6).
- The same spacing, radii, elevation, typography and chart-token shapes.
- The same `ThemeMode` / `ThemePreference` (`light` | `dark` | `system`)
  mechanism.
- The same **offline-first** discipline: local SQLite is the single source of
  truth; the app works fully without any network.
- The same architecture: Expo Router file-based routing, `src/database/` for
  all SQL, `src/services/` for pure logic, `src/ai/` for the only permitted
  AI transport, `src/data/DataProvider.tsx` owning app state.
- The same component vocabulary: `ScreenFade`, `KeyboardAwareScrollView`,
  `StatCard`, `AppDialog`, `EmptyState`, `FadeIn`, `AnimatedNumber`,
  `LoadingScreen`, `NumberField`, `OptionField`, `ScreenToast`,
  `CustomTabBar`.
- The same documentation convention: this file, `AGENTS.md`, `theme.md`,
  `README.md`, `release-plan.md`.
- The same **numbered-migration** schema discipline and the same repository
  API shape (`getX`, `getAllX`, `createX(input)`, `updateX`, `setXField`,
  `deleteX`, `upsertX`, `reorderX`).
- The same rule that the LLM **never writes to the database**. It returns
  candidates; the user and the service layer dispose.

### 2.2 What is deliberately different

- **Colour.** Coconut is shell brown, sage and dusty blue on warm cream. Cherry
  is coffee-brown and coffee-cherry red on oat-beige. Banana is ripe-banana
  gold and leaf-green on a fresh ivory paper, with a cool teal and a rum-amber
  as the third and fourth brand scales (`theme.md` §4, §5).
- **The unit of the app is a week, not a month and not a brew.** Coconut closes
  a month; Cherry records a brew; Banana plans a week and shops for it.
- **The fifth tab is Shopping, and the fourth is Deals.** The tabs are Plan,
  Recipes, Shopping, Deals, Settings.
- **Network is used for two things, and only two.** The first is the
  user-configured AI provider (`src/ai/`). The second is the **deal scraper**
  (`src/deals/`), which fetches current offers from retailer websites. This is
  a knowing, additional, and bounded exception to Coconut's and Cherry's
  "the only network call is the AI provider" rule — see §9. It is still
  local-first: every scraped deal is cached in SQLite, and the app works fully
  from the last snapshot with no network at all.
- **There is a pantry.** Cherry tracks remaining bean weight; Banana tracks what
  is already in the house, because that is what removes items from a shopping
  list.
- **Money is real, and central.** Prices, deals and an optional weekly budget
  are first-class. Money is stored as integer cents, exactly as in Coconut.

### 2.3 What is borrowed as an idea, not as code

Cherry's **Plan → Brew → Taste** lifecycle becomes Banana's
**Plan → Shop → Cook → Rate** lifecycle. Cherry's `extraction_runs` audit table
becomes Banana's `ai_runs`. Coconut's "the month is planned, judged and closed"
becomes "the week is planned, shopped and cooked". Coconut's integer-cents rule
is adopted wholesale.

## 3. The Core Model: A Week Has a Lifecycle

Coconut's strongest design decision is that a month is planned, judged and
closed — it does not simply pass. Banana applies the same idea to a week.

```
  SET UP            PLAN              SHOP             COOK            RATE
  budget            slots + meals     list              done marks      ratings
  time per day  ->  (deal-aware)  -> grouped,     ->  which meals -> verdicts
  do-not-want        proposals         de-duped,         were cooked     feed the
  want-to-eat                                            and leftovers   next plan
```

### 3.1 The Week

A **Week** is a committed intention for a seven-day span (Monday to Sunday by
default, configurable start day). It carries:

- `start_date` and `end_date`;
- a per-day **time budget** (§3.2);
- the chosen **slot layout** (breakfast, lunch and dinner by default, with
  snacks only when explicitly requested);
- an optional weekly **food budget** in cents;
- a `status`: `draft`, `planned`, `shopping`, `cooking`, `done`.

A Week is not a meal. It is the container. Creating a Week is always a
deliberate act.

### 3.2 Time budgets

For each meal on each day the user sets an independent **time budget**:
the number of minutes available to cook, or an explicit mode:

- `no_cook` — you are not cooking that day (takeaway, a restaurant, a fast);
- `leftovers` — you intend to eat the previous day's leftovers.
- `eating_out` — choose a restaurant or eating-out entry for this meal only.

`leftovers` is a first-class budget, not a note, because it is the single
biggest lever on both cost and time: it tells the planner to *cook once, eat
twice*. A plan that uses a `leftovers` day to earn a `cook once` is a good
plan.

The default is `45` minutes on every day, so a Week exists the moment it is
created and can be refined later.

### 3.3 Slots

A **slot** is one meal place: a day plus a meal type (`breakfast`, `lunch`,
`dinner`, `snack`). A slot holds either:

- a **recipe** chosen from the catalogue,
- a **free text** meal ("leftover curry", "at Mum's"),
- a reference to a **leftovers** budget, or
- nothing (unfilled).

Breakfast, lunch and dinner are always visible, including before the first
plan. Snacks are disabled until the user requests one for a particular day.
An unfilled slot is a legitimate, visible state — if no suitable recipe fits,
the app shows a gap rather than inventing a meal or choosing the wrong meal type.

### 3.4 Cook, rate, learn

A slot is marked **cooked** when the meal is made. Marking a slot cooked can
roll the surplus into a designated `leftovers` slot the next day, which is how
"cook once, eat twice" is recorded rather than merely hoped for.

Leftover meals can explicitly select an earlier, not-yet-cooked recipe meal in
the same week and home, including another meal type or a non-consecutive day.
The cooking meal records all portions to prepare; each linked leftover meal
records the portions to eat. Changing or removing a reservation adjusts the
cooking total atomically, and the shopping list counts ingredients only at the
cooking meal. Cooked/eaten batches cannot be resized or reassigned. The plan
visibly distinguishes cooking from leftovers, names the source day and meal,
and lists the portions reserved for each later meal. The existing consecutive-
days shortcut remains available when initially creating a batch; established
reservations are edited individually rather than silently rebuilt.

After cooking, a meal is **rated**: `liked` / `disliked` / `unsure`, plus an
optional 1–5 overall rating. Ratings are the learning signal. A disliked
recipe is deprecated for that household; a liked one is promoted.

## 4. Recipes and Ingredients

### 4.1 The recipe

A **recipe** is a reusable meal. It carries:

- `name`, `description`, `servings`;
- `prep_minutes`, `cook_minutes` (derived `total_minutes` is never stored);
- `effort` — `quick` / `normal` / `involved`, one coarse knob the planner uses
  before it reads minutes;
- `cuisine` (free text or a seeded list), `tags` (free-form: `comfort`,
  `one-pan`, `batch`, `spicy` …);
- `source` — `own`, `builtin`, `ai`;
- `rating` (derived from history, never stored — §13.3);
- `is_favourite`, `is_deprecated`;
- an ordered list of **recipe ingredients**;
- an ordered list of **steps**;
- one or more suitable meal tags (`breakfast`, `lunch`, `dinner`, `snack`),
  editable manually and extractable by AI; existing untagged recipes fall back
  to their original single category;
- an optional `eating_out` tag, for places such as "Pizza place". The planner
  can schedule these only for meals explicitly marked `eating_out`. They add
  no ingredients to the shopping list;
- `uuid` and `updated_at` from migration 1.

Recipe detail opens with the cooking steps expanded; the user can hide them.

### 4.2 Ingredients

An **ingredient** is a canonical pantry item, not a string on a recipe. It
carries a `name`, a `category` (an aisle: `produce`, `meat`, `dairy`, `bakery`,
`dry_goods`, `frozen`, `drinks`, `household`, `other`), a default `unit`, a
`is_staple` flag (salt, oil, flour), and any number of **aliases** ("spring
onion" ≈ "scallion" ≈ "Frühlingszwiebel").

Recipes reference ingredients by id and give the quantity as `quantity` (REAL)
plus a `unit` (`g`, `kg`, `ml`, `l`, `piece`, `tbsp`, `tsp`, `pinch`,
`to_taste`). A recipe ingredient may be marked `optional`, which keeps it off
the shopping list by default.

### 4.3 Pantry

The **pantry** records what is already at home: a chosen ingredient, an optional
approximate quantity and unit (for example, 500 g of butter), and a rough
confidence (`stocked` / `low` / `out`). Known amounts subtract from the combined
requirements for that home using compatible units. Without an amount, `stocked`
means enough and `low` means buy the required amount. `out` or absent means buy
it. The separate **Known ingredients** page lets the user add and edit names,
categories, default units, quantities, shelf life, staple status and notes.

The pantry is deliberately rough. It is not an inventory system; it is a
one-tap "we have this".

### 4.4 Preferences, diet and allergies

A single **preferences** record holds:

- `diet` — `omnivore` / `vegetarian` / `vegan` / `pescatarian`;
- `allergies` — a hard list of ingredient ids that must never appear;
- `dislikes` — a soft list, penalised but not forbidden;
- `default_servings`;
- `max_minutes_weeknight` — the ceiling the planner respects on school nights;
- `weekly_budget_cents` (optional);
- `household_name` (cosmetic).

**Allergies are absolute and are enforced in the service layer, not by the
LLM.** A recipe containing an allergen is filtered out before it is ever
offered, whatever the AI proposes.

## 5. Stores and Deals

### 5.1 Stores

A **store** is one of the supported retailers — seeded as **Lidl**, **Hofer**
( Aldi Süd in Austria) and **Spar** — each with a `slug`, a display name, a
base URL, and an `enabled` flag. The user can enable or disable a store, but
adding arbitrary stores is out of scope for v1.

### 5.2 Deals

A **deal** is one product on offer at one store for a validity window:

- `store_id`, `product_name`, `category` (the aisle it maps to, best-effort);
- `price_cents`, optional `was_price_cents`, optional `unit` and `quantity_text`
  ("1 kg", "500 g", "per piece");
- `valid_from`, `valid_to` (dates);
- `source_url`, `scraped_at`;
- `raw_text` — the line as the store published it, always kept so a bad parse
  is auditable.

Deals are shown in the Deals tab, grouped by store, sorted by validity and by
how much they touch the current plan.

### 5.3 Matching deals to ingredients

A **deal match** links a scraped deal product to a canonical ingredient, using
ingredient aliases and a normalised fuzzy match (never a naive substring). A
match has a `confidence` and is always **reviewable and correctable** by the
user: a wrong match is one tap to reject, and a rejected match is remembered.

A deal is **relevant** when its ingredient appears in the current plan or the
current shopping list. Relevance, not raw discount, is what the app surfaces:
"the plan calls for chicken; chicken is 30% off at Hofer this week."

### 5.4 The scraping boundary (see §9)

Scraping lives entirely in `src/deals/`, behind a `DealsProvider` interface
with one implementation per store plus a cache. It is:

- **best-effort** — a failed scrape never blocks the app and never destroys
  data; the previous snapshot simply persists;
- **cached** — every successful scrape is written to `deals` with its
  `scraped_at`, and the app reads from SQLite, never from the network, when
  rendering;
- **polite** — one request per store per refresh, an explicit `User-Agent`,
  a timeout, and a stale-after window (default 12 hours) so it is not hammered;
- **isolated** — parsers are pure functions `(html, store) => ParsedDeal[]`
  and are unit-tested against saved HTML fixtures, so the network never
  appears in a test.

## 6. The Shopping List

The shopping list is **derived, never stored as a hand-edited document** — with
one exception for items the user adds manually. It is computed from:

```
  sum of all recipe ingredients in the week's filled slots
    − known pantry quantities (or the whole requirement when stocked without an amount)
    − recipe ingredients marked optional (unless explicitly wanted)
    + manually added items
```

Concretely it:

1. **Aggregates** the same ingredient across recipes, converting units where it
   can and listing unconvertible ones separately rather than guessing.
2. **Groups** by store aisle/category, and orders the aisles in a sensible
   walking order.
3. **Annotates** each line with the best current deal for that ingredient
   across enabled stores, with the store name and the saving.
4. **Estimates** a total cost from deal prices where known and a fallback
   average where not, and compares it to the weekly budget (§3.1).
5. **Ticks off** as you shop; a ticked item can be added to the pantry in one
   tap ("I bought this, we now have it").

The list is one screen, one scroll, grouped headers, and a sticky total.

## 7. The AI Planner

AI is optional and entirely the user's: bring your own OpenAI-compatible
provider, base URL, key and model. It is the same transport as Cherry's
(`src/ai/provider.ts` → `openai-compatible.ts`).

The AI does **three** jobs, and never a fourth:

1. **Draft a plan.** Given the week's time budgets, preferences, allergies,
   dislikes, budget, the recipe catalogue, the pantry and the current deals,
   return a set of slot candidates: `{ day, meal_type, recipe_id | free_text,
   servings, reason }`. It must choose only recipes it was given; it may not
   invent a recipe, an ingredient id, or an allergen.
2. **Fill a gap.** Given one unfilled slot and the same context, propose a
   small set of candidate meals, each with a one-line reason.
3. **Narrate.** Given a plan already produced by the deterministic planner
   (§7.3), write a short, human rationale — "Tuesday is your long day, so it's
   a 20-minute one; you'll cook double and eat it again on Wednesday."

It never computes a deal match, never edits the shopping list, never overrides
an allergy, and never writes to the database.

### 6.1 Provider configuration

Identical to Cherry: base URL, API key and model are entered in the in-app AI
settings screen and stored only in the local `ai_state` singleton. They are
never hardcoded, never logged, never committed, and excluded from backups.

### 6.2 Structured output

The planner returns strict JSON, parsed, validated and repaired by
`src/ai/planner.ts` into candidates (`PlanCandidate[]`), exactly as Cherry's
extraction returns candidates. A candidate is not a plan; the plan screen and
the planning service dispose. Anything the model leaves uncertain goes into
`low_confidence`.

### 6.3 Degradation

There is always a **deterministic planner** (§7.3) that needs no network. If
the AI is unconfigured or unavailable, the deterministic planner still produces
a complete, valid plan, and a template writes the narrative. The AI is an
improvement, never a requirement.

## 7. Planning

### 7.1 Inputs

The planner receives: the week's days and their time budgets; the slot layout;
the preferences (diet, allergies, dislikes, default servings, weeknight
ceiling, budget); the recipe catalogue; the pantry; and the current deals.

### 7.4 Hard constraints and soft preferences

- **Hard** (never violated): allergies; `no_cook` and `leftovers` days; a day's
  available minutes for a `quick`/`normal` meal; servings positive.
- **Soft** (scored): using what is on offer; reusing ingredients across the
  week to shrink the shopping list; not repeating a cuisine two days running;
  honouring favourites; respecting dislikes; staying near the time budget
  rather than merely under it; staying under budget.

### 7.3 The deterministic planner

`src/services/planning-service.ts` is pure and deterministic. The same week,
catalogue, preferences, pins and recent-plan history produce the same result.
Pinned/cooked meals are reserved first, followed by the meals with the fewest
suitable choices, so a flexible day does not consume the only quick option.
Within eligible recipes, the least-used dishes take priority over repeats;
pantry/deal bonuses cannot overwhelm that variety preference. When choices are
limited, repeat counts are balanced and the proposal explains the limitation.
Near-identical ingredient combinations receive a soft penalty while useful
ingredient reuse, offers, pantry stock, ratings and expiry still contribute.

Cooking meals planned in the preceding four weeks receive a recency penalty
that decays with their scheduled meal dates. This works without marking meals
cooked or rated. Deleted meals/weeks and intentional leftover reservations do
not count as fresh cooking occurrences. Calendar-week-specific tie ranks avoid
permanently favouring low recipe ids without randomness. Existing pinned meals
and explicit leftover links are never rewritten just to improve variety.

The "Try something new" shortlist uses the same recent-plan cooldown and weekly
tie rotation, balances meal types and ingredient similarity, and excludes
allergies, incompatible diets, dislikes, inactive recipes and placeholder wishes.
All ranking and history calculations are local pure services; no derived score
or new tracking record is stored and no network request is added.

### 7.4 "Cook once, eat twice"

The planner actively tries to turn one cook into two meals across adjacent
days when a `leftovers` slot exists, because it is the biggest single saving on
time and cost. This is a scored objective, not a hard rule.

---

## 7.5 AI planner, precisely

The AI planner is invoked from the Plan screen's "Plan my week" action. It is
given the deterministic plan as a starting point and asked to improve it,
filling gaps and swapping for deals, within the constraints. Every change it
proposes is shown as a diff against the deterministic plan, and accepted or
rejected per change, never wholesale.

## 8. Main Screens

Five tabs and a small set of stack screens.

### Plan (home)

The current week at a glance: a card per day showing its time budget, its
meal(s) or a gap, and the leftovers link. The hero action is **Plan my week**
(deterministic, with an optional "ask AI to improve it"). A header shows the
week range, the estimated cost, and the budget bar. Below: this week's deals
that touch the plan.
Day cards can be expanded or collapsed from their headers. Dates before today
start collapsed; today and future dates start expanded. Headers, people/home
information and the day-edit shortcut stay visible. Manual expansion choices
are kept by calendar date while browsing weeks in the current screen session;
this is display state only and never changes the meal plan or shopping list.

### Recipes

The cookbook: your own recipes, plus the built-in starter set. Search, filter
by tag / cuisine / effort / max time, and sort by favourite and by rating.
Tapping a recipe opens its detail: ingredients, steps, history, and "add to
plan".

### Shopping

The derived list for the current week, grouped by aisle, ready to tick off.
A pinned header shows the estimated total against budget and the number of
deal matches. Manual items can be added. Buying a staple offers to update the
pantry.

### Deals

Scraped offers, grouped by store and filterable by aisle and by "relevant to my
plan". A refresh action triggers a scrape and shows the last-scraped time and
per-store result. Wrong ingredient matches are correctable here.

### Settings

Appearance (light / dark / system), household and diet, allergies and dislikes,
budget and default servings, week start day, stores on/off and scraping
preferences, AI provider, backup and restore, about and What's new.
Shops open in a separate settings page. Planning defaults and food preferences
share one **Preferences & defaults** subpage, including appearance/theme,
interface language and currency, to keep the main Settings page short.
The main page uses four card groups: Preferences & defaults with Shops;
Pantry and homes with Known ingredients; a rounded Check for updates toggle row
with SeedWorks account, AI provider and Dictation/model selection; and Backup
and restore with Tutorial, What's New and About. Check for updates is an inline
toggle, not navigation. Plan-update notifications have no in-app opt-out and
ignore obsolete saved disable values. Android notification permission remains
under the user's control and is requested when a plan update needs delivery,
not by opening Settings or at startup.

### Stack screens

`week/setup`, `slot/[id]`, `recipe/[id]`, `recipe/new`, `ingredient/[id]`,
`deals/match/[id]`, `pantry`, `ai-provider`, `planner` (the AI review), `backup`,
`tutorial`, `whats-new`, `about`.

## 9. Network, Privacy, and the Scraping Boundary

This is the section where Banana differs from its siblings, and it must be
honest about it.

**Two, and only two, kinds of outbound request exist.**

1. **The AI provider** (`src/ai/`) — the user's own base URL, key and model,
   invoked only when the user taps an AI feature. Identical in spirit to Cherry.
2. **The deal scraper** (`src/deals/`) — requests to the public web pages of
   the enabled retailers, to read their current offers. These requests carry no
   personal data, no identifiers and no account; they fetch a public page.

Everything else is local. There is no Banana backend, no account, no analytics,
no telemetry, no crash reporting, no advertising, and no third party.

Dictation offers the operating system's built-in on-device speech recognizer or
user-downloaded multilingual Whisper Base/Small Q5_1 models via `whisper.rn`
(user-approved local speech inference exception). Dictation settings select the
engine, model and spoken language independently from the interface language;
Whisper also supports automatic language detection. Whisper records first and
transcribes locally after Stop. Model downloads are explicit requests to the
public `ggerganov/whisper.cpp` Hugging Face repository and its CDN, with pinned
file sizes and SHA-256 verification. Models live in private Android no-backup
storage, survive normal updates, and are excluded from backups and household
sync. No model is automatically downloaded or replaced.
Banana does not bundle speech-model weights or upload audio. Leaflet recording
uses Expo Audio with Android's microphone foreground service and its recording
notification, so it can continue while another app is open. After Stop, Android
13+ (or Whisper on supported Android versions) transcribes the saved audio locally; the resulting text is sent to the
user-configured AI for leaflet extraction. Failed transcription keeps the audio
for retry; successful transcription or explicit cancellation removes it.

**Rules for the scraper, non-negotiable:**

- It only ever fetches a store's public offers page. It does not log in, does
  not submit forms, and does not follow the user anywhere.
- It respects `robots.txt` for the target path, and gives up gracefully when
  disallowed.
- It makes at most one request per store per refresh, with a timeout and a
  descriptive `User-Agent`, and never retries in a loop.
- It writes only to the local `deals` cache. A failed scrape is inert.
- It never sends the pantry, the plan, the budget, the user's location or any
  personal data to a retailer.
- Parsing is a pure function tested against fixtures; no test touches the
  network.

The user is responsible for how they use the app, and the app is designed to
be a well-behaved citizen: read the public page, cache the public facts, stay
quiet otherwise.

## 10. Notifications

Local only, sensitive, and off by default except the first:

1. **Plan the week** — a weekly nudge (default Sunday 17:00) when next week has
   no plan.
2. **Shop reminder** — when a week is `planned` and the shopping list is
   unstarted the day before its first cook day.
3. **Defrost / prep** — an optional reminder the evening before a slot whose
   recipe carries a `prep_ahead` tag.

---

## 11. Backup and Restore

Identical to Cherry and Coconut: export the whole SQLite file to a portable
document, or restore from one. **The API key is stripped before a backup
leaves the app.** Deals are transient and are exported too, but a restore never
depends on them.

## 12. Technical Stack

Mandatory, do not deviate without explicit user approval:

- React Native via **Expo SDK**
- **TypeScript** (strict mode)
- **expo-sqlite** for local storage
- **Expo Router** for navigation
- **React Native Paper** (Material Design 3) for UI
- **dayjs** for date handling
- **expo-notifications** for local notifications
- **expo-file-system** / **expo-sharing** for backup
- **@expo/vector-icons** for all icons
- **Jest** + jest-expo + @testing-library/react-native for tests
- **react-native-web** + react-dom for the desktop/web build

**No new dependency without explicit approval.** The scraper uses the platform
`fetch` and string parsing; it does not add an HTML-parsing library in the
foundation.

Archive and compression (for backup) are not allowed; keep the backup a single
SQLite file.

## 13. Data Model

### 13.1 Table inventory

Reference and configuration:

```

ai_state            singleton AI provider configuration
```

### 13.2 Conventions

- SQLite tables `snake_case` plural; columns `snake_case`, no type prefix.
- Money is `*_cents` INTEGER. Never a float.
- Durations are `*_minutes` INTEGER. Ingredient quantities are REAL. Physical
  measurements carry their unit in the column name (`quantity`, `unit`) or the
  suffix (`_minutes`, `_cents`).
- Dates are `TEXT` `YYYY-MM-DD`; timestamps are `TEXT` ISO-8601 with
  milliseconds and `Z`.
- Booleans are `INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0/1`.
- Enums are `TEXT` with `CHECK (col IS NULL OR col IN (...))`.
- Foreign keys: `ON DELETE CASCADE` for true children, `ON DELETE SET NULL` for
  references that must survive.
- One repository module per aggregate; a trailing `db?: SQLiteDatabase` on
  every function so transactions reuse one handle.

### 13.3 Derived, never stored

Derived values are **never** columns. If you find yourself adding one, stop.
The derived values include: a recipe's `total_minutes`, its `rating` and
cook-count from history, a week's estimated cost, its budget variance, the
shopping list itself, deal savings, and pantry sufficiency. They are computed
in `src/services/` on demand.

### 13.4 No sync in v1

Coconut has optional Supabase sync and Shared Spaces. Banana does not in v1.
The `uuid` columns keep the door open, exactly as Cherry's do.

## 14. Design Philosophy

1. **Planning a week must be faster than not planning it**, or the app has
   failed. The final state of a Sunday evening should be: open the app, glance,
   tap once, have a plan.
2. **A plan is a proposal, never a command.** Every AI or deterministic choice
   can be changed, and every change is remembered.
2. **The shopping list is the deliverable.** A plan you cannot shop for is a
   wish. The list is one screen, one aisle order, one total.
3. **Allergies are sacred.** The LLM never overrides them; a human can, per
   item, but the default is absolute.
4. **Nothing is invented.** Gaps are shown as gaps. A day with no cooking is a
   valid day.
4. **Cook once, eat twice** is the default aspiration wherever leftovers make
   sense.
5. **Spend where the plan wants it.** A deal is only interesting if the plan
   needs it; deals do not drive the plan, they inform it.
6. **The network is a guest.** It may read a public offers page; it may call the
   user's own AI. It may do nothing else. Everything works offline.
